The Return

Most of us did not leave Bengal. We left a village, and Bengal happened to be around it.

There is a word for the place — vite. The bati. The plot the family held, the pond behind it, the name of the block you still give when an elder asks where you are really from. You may not have stood on it in thirty years. You can still draw it.

The young people left, as you left. The land stayed, and got smaller with each division. The ones who remained were told, in a hundred quiet ways, that farming was what you did when nothing else worked out.

Agriculture in Bengal did not fail for want of soil, or rain, or hands. It failed for want of prestige.

That is why this takes the shape of a league rather than a scheme. A subsidy makes a farmer solvent for a year. A championship makes farming something a young person in Bengal might actually want.

Pratyaavartan means homecoming — the idea that a place can find its way back to the direction it had, and that the people who left are not disqualified from helping.

The mechanism

The Krishi Ratna League

Take the format that made a nation watch cricket for two months a year, and point it at farming.

West Bengal's 294 assembly constituencies are grouped into roughly 15 regional teams — a franchise here is a group of constituencies, not a cricket side. Every farm carries its own marks; a team's score is the aggregate of its farms. There is a table, and it moves.

Economic viability, productivity, sustainable income from whatever resources exist.

Qualification gate: a farm must be 100% chemical and pesticide free to be scored at all. A jury grades the farms; AI-assisted monitoring supports the farmer and records progress through the year.

Who stands behind it

Seven decades, and eighteen years

Bharatiya Krishak Samaj

Founded 3 April 1955 by Dr Panjabrao S. Deshmukh, independent India's first Union Agriculture Minister, who kept it deliberately outside party politics so it could argue with any government of the day.

National President Shri Krishan Bir Chaudhary sits on the Government of India's Minimum Support Price Committee. He is a former Chairman of the State Farms Corporation of India and of the Indian Sugarcane Development Council, a founder of SFAC and a former director of NAFED.

Its national advisory board includes Dr R.S. Paroda (former Director General, ICAR), Siraj Hussain IAS (former Secretary, Ministry of Agriculture), Ashish Bahuguna IAS (former Chairman, FSSAI), Dr Baldev Singh Dhillon (former VC, Punjab Agricultural University) and Gopa Sabharwal (former VC, Nalanda University).

KarmYog for the 21st Century

Founded in 2008 by Mahacharya Sourabh J. Sarkar — B.Tech, IIT Kharagpur; M.A. Education & New Media, Syracuse University. Its subject has always been the same one: how a population changes a habit.

Kolkata Jaago — Safe Drive Save Life reached 300+ schools and roughly one lakh taxi drivers in Kolkata. Jeevan-Jeevika Abhiyaan changed farming practice in Odisha through the SRI technique, and worked with informal-sector workers in Rajasthan, health workers in Howrah and safai karmacharis in Mumbai.

OmniDEL won the NSDC Best Learning Methodology Award in 2014 and a million-dollar grant from TATA Trusts in 2015. It is now at the forefront of Agentic AI for informal-sector transformation — the capability this League's verification layer is built on.

The uncomfortable section

What we promise, and what we do not

"How do I know the farm is real?"

Because you will watch it. Farm activity is logged with dated photographs and video. Expenses are recorded against that activity and published to a ledger you can open. AI processes the volume and flags what does not reconcile; human corrections stay visible rather than overwriting the record.

The principle the platform is built on is one sentence: what happens on the farm should be documentable and traceable.

"What if the farm fails?"

Then it fails, and you will see that happen too. This is a real farm, run by a real young person, against weather and pests and markets and their own inexperience. The farmer will do his best and so will we. We cannot guarantee success — no one honestly can.

What we do guarantee is that a struggling farm will not quietly disappear from your dashboard. You will see the bad month, and what was tried.

Supporters holding foreign passports or OCI cards — please read

KarmYog for the 21st Century Foundation holds 80G but does not yet hold FCRA registration, which is what Indian law requires before an organisation may receive a contribution from a foreign source. Under FCRA, an OCI cardholder or foreign passport holder is treated as a foreign source regardless of where they were born.

So at this moment the invitation is to resident Indians and to Non-Resident Indians holding Indian passports. If you hold a foreign passport or an OCI card, please register your interest — FCRA registration is being pursued and we will come back to you the moment it is in place. We would rather tell you this here than take your money incorrectly.
