Page 2 — Executive Summary: "The whole of it, briefly"

If you read nothing else, read this page. Everything after it is evidence for what is written here.

WHAT IT IS — The Krishi Ratna League is a championship for farms, run in the format that made cricket unmissable. Across West Bengal's 294 assembly constituencies, 5,000 young women and men will each build a Smart Integrated Farm — modern technique on small land — and be mentored, verified and graded through one full season, Durga Puja 2026 to Durga Puja 2027.

YOUR PART — You stand behind one of them. Not a fund, not a cause — one named young person, in a district you choose. You will be shown three applicants and you pick who you back.

THE AMOUNT — ₹1,00,000 for the season, paid as five monthly instalments of ₹20,000. Of it, ₹90,000 goes to the farm. ₹10,000 — ten per cent — runs the League: verification, mentoring, the technology platform and the prize pool.

WHAT YOU SEE — A named farm and farmer. An online dashboard with the activity log, photographs and the expense ledger. A progress report against every instalment. The farmer's own video messages. And an invitation to the Season 1 awards at Durga Puja 2027.

WHO STANDS BEHIND IT — Bharatiya Krishak Samaj, founded 3 April 1955 by Dr Panjabrao S. Deshmukh, independent India's first Union Agriculture Minister — seven decades for the Indian farmer. Its National President, Shri Krishan Bir Chaudhary, sits on the Government of India's Minimum Support Price Committee. Delivery is by KarmYog for the 21st Century, which has run culture-change campaigns at state scale for eighteen years.

THE PRIZES — ₹3 crore in all — ₹1 crore for the best farm, ₹1 crore for the best regional team, ₹1 crore across other categories. Farms are graded on four dimensions: Satya, Mangal, Sundar, Samriddhi.

THE CALENDAR — Launched 14 September 2026 by the Hon'ble Minister of Agriculture, Government of West Bengal. Public opening at the Durga Puja from 16 October 2026, whose ground is itself the first model farm. Season closes at Durga Puja 2027 with the awards and the All-India launch.

THE NEXT STEP — A twenty-minute call. No commitment is asked on it. You will be shown the model farm, the dashboard and three applicants from your district.

Figures: ₹10 crore to be raised before Durga Puja 2026 — one thousand farms. 5,000 Smart Integrated Farms across Bengal in Season 1.

Page 3 — The Return (Pratyaavartan)

Most of us did not leave Bengal. We left a village, and Bengal happened to be around it.

There is a word for the place — vite. The bati. The plot the family held, the pond behind it, the name of the block you still give when an elder asks where you are really from. You may not have stood on it in thirty years. You can still draw it.

What has happened to those places is not a mystery and it is not anybody's single fault. The young people left, as you left. The land stayed, and got smaller with each division. The ones who remained were told, in a hundred quiet ways, that farming was what you did when nothing else worked out. A boy who wanted to stay had no reason he could say out loud.

PULL QUOTE: Agriculture in Bengal did not fail for want of soil, or rain, or hands. It failed for want of prestige.

That is the specific thing this programme sets out to change, and it is why it takes the shape of a league rather than a scheme. A subsidy makes a farmer solvent for a year. A championship makes farming something a young person in Bengal might actually want — with a team, a table, a season, a name that gets read out.

Pratyaavartan means homecoming. Not the aeroplane kind. It is the idea that a place can find its way back to the direction it had, and that the people who left it are not disqualified from helping — that in fact they may be the only ones with both the distance to see it clearly and the means to do something.

You are not being asked for charity. Charity flows down. This is closer to what an elder in the family used to do for a promising nephew — you back the young person, you stay interested, you ask how it went. The money matters. Being watched by someone who left and still cares matters more than we can put in a table.

NOTE — What this is not: This is not a donation to a general fund, and you are not buying a product. You are supporting one named agri-entrepreneur who is setting up a Smart Integrated Farm, and you will be able to see, month by month, exactly what happened on that piece of land.

Page 4 — Why this year and not another

Three things have come together in 2026 that have not been true at the same time before.

One — the governments are aligned. For the first time in nearly five decades, the government in West Bengal and the government at the Centre are working in the same direction. Whatever one thinks of any party — and Bharatiya Krishak Samaj takes no position on any — agricultural reform is one of the few fields where that alignment translates directly into what a farmer can actually reach: schemes that arrive, approvals that move, institutions that answer. A window like this stays open for a few years, not forever. The work should be done inside it.

Two — agriculture never got its league. Cricket had the IPL. Music had Sa Re Ga Ma Pa. Dance had Dance India Dance. Each of them changed what an ordinary family thought was a respectable thing for a child to spend a decade on. Agriculture — the one occupation that feeds the rest — has never had its equivalent. No table, no season, no tournament, no moment where the country watched. That absence is not a small thing. It is most of the reason a talented nineteen-year-old in Bankura does not consider the land in front of him.

Three — the technology finally makes it honest. A programme like this was not practical ten years ago, because it could not be watched. A supporter in Bengaluru had no way to know whether a farm eight hundred kilometres away existed, or whether the money reached the soil. That has changed. Farm activity can be logged with photographs and video, dated and located. Expenses can be recorded against activity and published to a ledger. AI can process the volume of evidence that five thousand farms will generate and flag what does not reconcile. This is the first year one could promise a supporter continuous sight of a farm and actually mean it.

PANEL — The deadline is not ours: Durga Puja is a fixed date. It cannot be pushed for a delayed approval or a slow quarter. That is precisely why the whole three-year journey is pinned to it — Puja 2026 to launch, Puja 2027 to award, 2028 for what Bengal has become. A project without a non-negotiable milestone drifts. This one cannot.

Page 5 — Bharatiya Krishak Samaj

On 3 April 1955, Dr Panjabrao S. Deshmukh — independent India's first Union Minister of Agriculture — founded a farmers' body and deliberately kept it outside party politics, so that it could argue with any government of the day.

Seven decades later it is one of India's leading non-partisan farmers' organisations. It is not a political party and it is not a government scheme. It has spent that time in the unglamorous middle ground: on committees, in consultations, in the drafting of the rules that decide what a farmer is paid.

National President — Shri Krishan Bir Chaudhary: Member, Minimum Support Price Committee, Government of India — the body that advises on the prices at which the country procures its food. Former Chairman, State Farms Corporation of India. Former Chairman, Indian Sugarcane Development Council, Government of India. Founder of the Small Farmers' Agribusiness Consortium (SFAC). Former Director, NAFED.

The National Advisory Board — the people who advise this organisation are the people who used to run Indian agriculture:

  • Dr R.S. Paroda — Former Director General, Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR)
  • Siraj Hussain, IAS (Retd.) — Former Secretary, Ministry of Agriculture, Government of India
  • Ashish Bahuguna, IAS (Retd.) — Former Chairman, Food Safety and Standards Authority of India
  • Dr Baldev Singh Dhillon — Vice Chancellor (Retd.), Punjab Agricultural University
  • Gopa Sabharwal — Vice Chancellor (Retd.), Nalanda University

The West Bengal Chapter: Established in June 2026 at the direction of the National President. Mahacharya Sourabh J. Sarkar was appointed State President on 1 September 2026, and Mrs Rinku Majumder Ghosh Head of the Mahila Mandal on the same date. The Krishi Ratna League is the chapter's first major undertaking.

Page 6 — KarmYog for the 21st Century

Bharatiya Krishak Samaj brings seven decades of standing with the Indian farmer. KarmYog brings the thing that is harder to buy: a record of actually changing how large numbers of people behave.

KarmYog was founded in 2008 by Mahacharya Sourabh J. Sarkar — B.Tech, IIT Kharagpur, 1990; M.A. in Education and New Media, S.I. Newhouse School, Syracuse University — who left a corporate career to build it. Its subject has always been the same one: how a population changes a habit.

Kolkata Jaago — Safe Drive Save Life: Road-safety culture change across the city. Reached 300+ schools and roughly one lakh taxi drivers in Kolkata. This is the closest precedent to what the Krishi Ratna League has to do: take a behaviour everybody defends out of habit, and shift it — at the scale of a state, in Bengali, through the people who already have the trust.

Jeevan-Jeevika Abhiyaan — farmers, already: Mass behaviour-change campaigns for farmers in Odisha through the SRI farming technique; informal-sector workers in Rajasthan; vocational trainees in Maharashtra; health workers in Howrah; safai karmacharis in Mumbai. The Odisha work matters most here. Persuading farmers to change a planting method they inherited is the same problem as KRL, solved once already.

OmniDEL — recognised, then funded: NSDC Best Learning Methodology Award, 2014. A million-dollar grant from TATA Trusts, 2015. Today OmniDEL is at the forefront of Agentic AI for informal-sector transformation — which is the capability the League's verification and transparency layer is built on.

Growing things, at scale, on bad land: KarmYog Green Village, 2015 to date: roughly 12 acres of HIDCO wasteland turned into a working eco-village — 2,000+ fruit-bearing trees, pesticide-free vegetable production, training facilities. 50+ biophilic designs and 7+ Vatikas built across Kolkata, Patna and Goa. Named a biophilic pioneer in India at ABID Expo 2024.

NOTE — Why this pairing is the point: A farmers' organisation with seven decades of institutional standing, and a movement with eighteen years of getting ordinary people to do something differently. Either one alone has tried this before and stalled. The reason to believe the Krishi Ratna League will run is that both halves of the problem have an owner.

Page 7 — The Krishi Ratna League

Take the format that made a nation watch cricket for two months a year, and point it at farming.

West Bengal's 294 assembly constituencies are grouped into roughly 15 regional teams — a franchise here is a group of constituencies, not a cricket side. Selected young farmers in each constituency build their farms through the season. Every farm carries its own marks; a team's score is the aggregate of its farms. There is a table, and it moves.

How a farm is graded:

  • Satya (Truth) — 30%. Organic cultivation. No chemical dependency, no pesticides. Transparent, dated evidence of what was actually done on the land.
  • Mangal (Goodness) — 25%. Good for human health, for the soil, for the surrounding environment and its ecosystem — not only for the yield.
  • Sundar (Beauty) — 20%. Natural aesthetic quality, considered design, harmony with the land. A farm should be a place one would want to stand in.
  • Samriddhi (Prosperity) — 25%. Economic viability, productivity, sustainable income, efficient use of whatever resources the farmer actually has.

Qualification gate: a farm must be 100% chemical and pesticide free to be scored at all. AI-assisted monitoring supports the farmer through the year and records progress; a jury grades the farms.

The prize pool: Best individual farm — the most prosperous farm in Bengal — ₹1,00,00,000. Best regional team — highest aggregate across 300–400 farms — ₹1,00,00,000. Category awards — innovation and other categories — ₹1,00,00,000. Total prize pool, Season 1: ₹3,00,00,000.

The team prize exists on purpose: a farmer in Purulia has a reason to help a farmer two constituencies away, because their marks are added together. The League is built to produce cooperation, not only winners.

Page 8 — The Smart Integrated Farm

Old Indian practice, mixed with modern technology, on land small enough that an ordinary family actually has it.

The central claim of the Krishi Ratna League is not that Bengal needs bigger farms. It is that a prosperous farm can run on one to four bigha if the design is right — integrated so that each element feeds another, organic by requirement, instrumented so the farmer can see what is working, and planned for income rather than subsistence.

Saying "I am farming" is not enough to be selected. The applicant has to design something. That is the filter, and it is deliberately a high one.

Munshi Bheri — the proof, built first: Before asking five thousand young people to do it, we are doing it once, in public, on the worst possible land. Munshi Bheri, in the East Kolkata Wetlands, was a dump yard. Over roughly six weeks it is being turned into the first model Smart Integrated Farm — about 1.5 acres, four and a half to five bigha. It is a learning farm: everything on it is meant to be copied.

And it is the Durga Puja ground. The Durga Puja 2026 theme is Krishi Samaj — for the farmers — and the pandal stands on the farm itself. Lakhs of people will walk through a working integrated farm during the Puja without having planned to learn anything. That is the awakening — Jagaran — and it is the reason the League launches at a Puja and not at a conference.

PANEL — Jagaran → Jijnasa → Adhyayan → Sadhana: Awakening leads to a question. The question leads to study. Study becomes disciplined practice. It is the sequence the whole three-year journey is built on, and it is why the Puja ground had to be a real farm rather than a decorated one — a person who walks through and asks how has already taken the first step.

NOTE — Said plainly: Some of the League's finer structure is still being settled — the exact number of farms per constituency, the final team map, the detailed marking rubric and the jury. Those are marked as not final wherever they appear, here and on the website, and they will be published when they are fixed rather than described before.

Page 9 — What supporting one farm means

One supporter. One agri-entrepreneur. One season. ₹1,00,000.

  • Total support for one farm, one season — ₹1,00,000
  • Paid as five monthly instalments of ₹20,000 — 5 × ₹20,000
  • To the farm — seed capital for the young entrepreneur — ₹90,000
  • To the League — verification, mentoring, technology, prize pool — ₹10,000
  • Proportion reaching the land — 90%

We would rather print the ten per cent than bury it. Running a verified championship across 294 constituencies costs something, and a supporter is entitled to know exactly what. Ten per cent is what it costs. The remaining ninety goes into the ground.

How you choose: You tell us the district — normally the one your family comes from. We send you three applicants from it, with their profiles and what each intends to build. You pick one. If your district has more supporters than applicants in a given round, we will say so and offer you the neighbouring blocks rather than quietly moving you.

What you receive, monthly: A named farm and the farmer's profile. An online dashboard — activity log, photographs, expense ledger. Progress reported against each of the five instalments. The farmer's own video messages. Invitation to the Season 1 awards, Durga Puja 2027. A certificate of support; the farm may carry your vite, or a parent's name.

If you want to go further: Some supporters will want to do more than one season with a young person who is clearly good. A path from support into a genuine partnership or investment is being designed. It has no terms yet, and we will not invent any here. When it exists, you will be among the first told.

You may support more than one farm: Several supporters have asked about backing a group of farms in one block, or a whole panchayat. That is possible and it is worth a conversation rather than a price list — the practicalities differ by district. Raise it on the call.

Page 10 — What we promise, and what we do not

Everyone who has been asked for money from abroad, or from the next city, has the same two questions. Here they are answered before you ask them.

"How do I know the farm is real?" Because you will watch it. Farm activity is logged with dated photographs and video. Expenses are recorded against that activity and published to a ledger you can open. AI processes the volume and flags what does not reconcile; human corrections stay visible rather than overwriting the record. Every rupee has a record, every record has a date, and the farm's own evidence sits next to its accounts. The principle the platform is built on is one sentence: what happens on the farm should be documentable and traceable.

"What if the farm fails?" Then it fails, and you will see that happen too. This is a real farm, run by a real young person, against weather and pests and markets and their own inexperience. The farmer will do his best and so will we. We cannot guarantee success — no one honestly can. Anyone who offers you a guaranteed agricultural outcome is either not telling you the truth or has not farmed. What we do guarantee is that a struggling farm will not quietly disappear from your dashboard. You will see the bad month. You will see what was tried. A supporter who is told only the good news has not been given transparency, only marketing — and this programme is going to be asking people for money for three years, which is far too long to be caught doing that.

Where the money sits: Funds are received by KarmYog for the 21st Century Foundation, which holds 80G registration. Bharatiya Krishak Samaj and KarmYog are jointly responsible for evaluating applicants so that the money is not wasted — that evaluation is the main thing the ten per cent buys.

NOTE — Supporters holding foreign passports or OCI cards, please read: The Foundation currently holds 80G but does not yet hold FCRA registration, which is what Indian law requires before an organisation may receive a contribution from a foreign source. Under FCRA, an OCI cardholder or foreign passport holder is treated as a foreign source regardless of where they were born. So at this moment the invitation in this document is to resident Indians and to Non-Resident Indians holding Indian passports. If you hold a foreign passport or an OCI card, please register your interest — FCRA registration is being pursued and we will come back to you the moment it is in place. We would rather tell you this on page ten than take your money incorrectly.

There is only one thing to do next, and it costs twenty minutes.

1. Book a twenty-minute call. No commitment is asked on it. Tell us the district your family comes from. We will walk you through the model farm at Munshi Bheri, open the dashboard in front of you, and answer whatever you want to press on.
